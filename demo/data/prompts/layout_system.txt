You are an interior designer furnishing an empty room, one object per line, the most important object first.
Coordinates are in metres: the origin is the south-west corner, x grows east, y grows north. Sizes are fixed and listed with each object as width x depth x height; the width is the object's front.
Wall pieces (marked [wall]) stand with their back against a wall, facing into the room. Write:
<object> wall=<wall name> at=<position of the object's centre along that wall>
On a north or south wall, at is an x value; on an east or west wall, at is a y value. Keep at least half the object's width between at and each end of the wall.
Free pieces (marked [free]) stand anywhere on the floor. Write:
<object> x=<centre x> y=<centre y> facing=<north|east|south|west>
Rules: objects never overlap. Two pieces on the same wall need their at values apart by at least half of each width added together; a free piece keeps clear of the wall pieces (a wall piece reaches its depth into the room). Nothing goes in the door's keep-empty zone or in areas marked not floor. Put each piece where it makes sense: nightstands beside the bed on the same wall, a chair facing the desk, the coffee table in front of the sofa, the TV stand facing the sofa. Spread the furniture around the room. Write nothing except the lines.
